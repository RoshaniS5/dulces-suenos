I first knew something was wrong with me when I was four.

It was the middle of the night, and I was barefoot in the bathroom, my hands entangled beneath the warm tap water as I washed the last of the suds off. As I looked up from the bubbles snaking their way down the sides of the basin and to the drain,

I saw my reflection in the mirror

contort itself into a smile without my permission. As I slowly stepped backwards, I couldn’t place my finger on what exactly was wrong besides that smile, but she didn’t look like me.
